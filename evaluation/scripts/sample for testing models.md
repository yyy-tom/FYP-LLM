# **Background 1 – Low Mood & Motivation**

### **Patient Name: Emily, 20**

### **Overall Situation**

Emily is a second-year university student who has been struggling for the past month with persistent low mood, fatigue, and a strong sense of heaviness. She finds it difficult to get out of bed in the morning, often lying there for hours scrolling on her phone. Tasks that used to be enjoyable—like drawing and biking—now feel pointless. She feels guilty for “being lazy,” even though she knows something deeper is going on.

Her appetite has decreased, she sleeps irregularly, and she often tells herself that she’s “not doing enough.” She hasn’t told many friends because she’s afraid they’ll think she’s dramatic. She wants to improve, but she doesn’t know where to start, and that increases her hopelessness.

### **Patient’s Emotional Patterns**

* Frequently criticizes herself for lacking motivation.  
* Feels “numb” or empty rather than overwhelmed.  
* Worried things will “always be this way.”  
* Wants help but is afraid of being a burden.

### **What the patient tends to say in dialogues**

When replying to a chatbot, Emily often explains:

* Why she lacks motivation (“even simple things feel heavy”).  
* What she tried but didn't work (“I tried making a plan but I couldn’t follow it”).  
* How she feels emotionally and physically (“I feel drained even before I do anything”).  
* Her fears about the future.

### **How to modify Emily’s replies to fit chatbot questions**

* **If the chatbot asks about triggers →** Add details about when the low mood worsens (e.g., morning, after class).  
* **If the chatbot asks about coping →** Mention small attempts (like walks, journaling) and why they felt difficult.  
* **If the chatbot asks how it affects life →** Add examples like missing classes or neglecting self-care.  
* **If chatbot asks what she hopes for →** Mention she wants to feel lighter, more in control, or able to enjoy hobbies again.

# **Background 2 – Academic Stress & Depressive Feelings**

### **Patient Name: Daniel, 21**

### **Overall Situation**

Daniel is a computer science major facing a difficult semester with heavy coursework in algorithms, systems, and a group project where others aren’t contributing. He has been losing confidence because his grades dropped recently. He studies long hours but constantly feels he’s not doing enough. He often compares himself to classmates, especially those who seem to succeed effortlessly.

He experiences guilt when resting and constantly cycles between stress and exhaustion. He sometimes feels on the edge of burnout—tight chest, headaches, difficulty concentrating, and waking up at night with thoughts racing about deadlines. He doesn’t want to disappoint his parents, who work hard to support him.

### **Patient’s Emotional Patterns**

* High pressure and perfectionism.  
* Tendency to catastrophize (“If I fail this exam, everything will fall apart”).  
* Often feels overwhelmed and mentally exhausted.  
* Internal conflict: wants help but feels he "should handle it himself."

### **What the patient tends to say in dialogues**

* Explains sources of stress (“assignments piling up,” “project group not cooperating”).  
* Talks about negative thoughts (“everyone else seems to understand faster than me”).  
* Mentions physical stress symptoms.  
* Expresses fear of failure and letting others down.

### **How to modify Daniel’s replies to fit chatbot questions**

* **If chatbot asks for reasons for stress →** Add details about deadlines, projects, or comparisons.  
* **If chatbot asks about feelings →** Expand on the emotional impact—frustration, pressure, self-doubt.  
* **If chatbot asks how he copes →** Mention both unhealthy (overworking, skipping breaks) and healthy attempts (planning, visiting library).  
* **If chatbot asks what he wants →** Mention wanting balance, confidence, and manageable workload.

# **Background 3 – Relationship Struggles & Sadness**

### **Patient Name: Sara, 19**

### **Overall Situation**

Sara recently ended a year-long relationship with her boyfriend. The breakup wasn’t explosive, but deeply painful—he said he felt they were “growing apart.” She still cares about him and feels confused because they had many future plans together. She keeps replaying their final conversations in her head, wondering if she caused the breakup or could have prevented it.

Sara feels lonely, especially at night. She avoids mutual friends because talking about him makes her tearful. She has lost interest in social activities and feels emotionally fragile. Sometimes she texts him but regrets it afterward. She’s afraid she won’t find someone who understands her the same way again.

### **Patient’s Emotional Patterns**

* Rumination and self-blame.  
* Waves of sadness triggered unexpectedly.  
* Fear of rejection or not being “good enough.”  
* Difficulty letting go and moving forward.

### **What the patient tends to say in dialogues**

* Talks about what the relationship meant to her.  
* Expresses confusion about the breakup.  
* Describes loneliness and emotional pain.  
* Wonders how to heal or stop thinking about him.

### **How to modify Sara’s replies to fit chatbot questions**

* **If chatbot asks how she feels →** Describe specific emotional waves (e.g., crying at night, numb during the day).  
* **If chatbot asks what she misses →** Mention specific moments, routines, or emotional support.  
* **If chatbot asks about coping attempts →** Talk about texting him, journaling, or trying to distract herself.  
* **If chatbot asks what she fears →** Mention fear of being unlovable or not finding someone else.
